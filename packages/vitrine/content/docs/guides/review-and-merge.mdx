title: Review and merge
description: Try each story's work in its running app, then request changes, reject it, or approve and merge.
---

The agent moves fast; the decision stays yours. Nothing merges until you approve it.

## Review a story

When an agent hands its work back, the story moves to **In Review**. Open it:

- **Preview** runs the story's own copy of the app: try the change there.
- **Code → Diff** shows what changed, and **Files** and **Terminal** let you look around the worktree.
- **User Story** has the brief and the agent's conversation and activity. Before handing the work back, the agent runs its required checks again (see [the Light Semantic Harness](/docs/concepts/light-semantic-harness)).

## Decide

| Action              | What happens                                                                                   |
| ------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Request Changes** | Write a comment: the story goes back to **In Progress** and the agent takes another pass.      |
| **Reject**          | Choose **Request Changes** after all, or **Discard User Story**, which moves it to **Discarded**. Discarding a Direct story keeps the files the agent changed in your project folder. |
| **Approve & Merge** | Blame merges the story's branch into your local branch, without pushing, and the story moves to **Done**. The arrow next to the button offers **Approve, Merge & Push**, which also pushes the branch, and **Open PR**. The same button and menu sit on the story's card in the backlog. |
| **Approve & Commit** | A Direct story, which already works in your project folder, has nothing to merge: Blame commits its files on the checked-out branch, and the story moves to **Done**. |

The approve button reads **Mark as Done** when the story changed nothing.

## What Approve & Commit does

Your project folder is shared with your own work and with other Direct stories, chats and Plan Mode, so Blame commits only the files this story wrote:

1. It finds the files the agent wrote for this story that still differ from the last commit. Your other uncommitted changes stay uncommitted, and files you already staged stay staged and out of the commit.
2. If one of those files also holds changes that are not the story's, such as a file you had modified before the story started, Blame lists them and asks: commit them with the story, leave them out, or cancel.
3. An agent writes the commit message, with your **Alternative model** and your project's commit convention.
4. Blame commits on the checked-out branch and keeps the commit on the story, so its diff stays visible once it is **Done**. Nothing is pushed.

If none of the story's changes are left in the folder, or the commit fails, the story stays in review and Blame says why.

## What a merge does

Blame merges into the branch checked out in your project folder:

1. If that branch has an upstream, Blame pulls it first.
2. It commits the story's work with a message written by an agent.
3. It brings the story's branch up to date with yours, then fast-forwards your branch to it.
4. Blame does not push. With **Approve, Merge & Push**, if your branch has an upstream, Blame pushes it.

<Callout title="Merging does not push">
  Approve & Merge, and dragging a story to **Done**, leave the merged result on your local branch: your branch ends up ahead of its upstream. Choose **Approve, Merge & Push** in the menu next to the button to push it to the branch's upstream in the same step. If the branch has no upstream, nothing is pushed and Blame offers to publish the branch after the merge.
</Callout>

Merge conflicts are resolved by an agent, with up to three attempts; if conflicts remain, the merge stops and says so. Uncommitted changes in your project folder are set aside during the merge and put back afterwards. If they conflict with the story's changes, the merge stops before your branch moves.

The merge runs with your **Alternative model**, set in **Settings → Models & Providers**.

## After the merge

- A **Done** story can start a follow-up: **New story with this context**.
- A **Discarded** story can come back with **Rework as Draft**, or go for good with **Delete Permanently**.
